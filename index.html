Point of View

A ghost note is
felt more than heard.

It is a real term. In music, a ghost note is played so lightly it barely registers as a note at all. You do not hear it. You feel it. Take it out and the whole groove goes flat, and most people cannot tell you why.

That is the idea Ghost Note is built on, and it is why this particular brief landed the way it did with us.

You have 3,300 people. Roughly 2,300 of them have gone quiet. They did not resign. They did not object. They did not send a note explaining themselves. They simply stopped registering, and the program kept playing without noticing the groove had gone flat.

Here is the thing worth sitting with. Those people did not stop caring about workforce equity. Many of them have moved into bigger roles since they last heard from you, built teams, taken on exactly the mandate Expanding Equity was designed to support. They stopped hearing from a program that was busy recruiting the next group.

Silence is not rejection. It is just silence, and it can be answered.

So we would not frame Welcome Back as a re-announcement. Re-announcing assumes the problem is that people forgot. We do not think they forgot. We think they stopped being reached, which is a different problem with a different solution.

And the solution is not a louder send. It is a system that knows the difference between a person who just finished the Lab, a person who attended one panel in 2021, and a person who has not been spoken to since before the world changed.

Getting that difference right is what makes a program feel like it was built for you rather than sent to you. That is resonance. It is the thing we build.

One more thing about how we work, because it changes what this costs. FSG has already done the strategy. You ran a refresh in April. Teal Media completed brand messaging in June. That work is recent and good enough to build on, so we are not proposing to do it again. There is no discovery phase in here. We are lifting what exists and putting it in front of your network. On a $100,000 budget, a strategy phase you do not need is the difference between launching in September and launching in November.

What This Work Is

Not a design project
with email attached.

This is the section we'd most want you to read. "Communication strategy and assets" makes this sound like a creative scope with some sending on the end. It isn't, and pricing it that way is how these programs fail quietly.

Sending email is a campaign. Lifecycle is a system.

Someone writes a campaign, someone designs it, someone hits send, and it goes to a list. Lifecycle decides on its own who receives what, when, and why, based on what each person has done or not done. Nobody hits send. The system reads a member's state and responds to it.

That distinction is the whole ballgame for a network like yours. Sending three very different people the same thing is why 70 percent went dormant in the first place.

Industry benchmark data is unambiguous here: automated sequences segmented by history and lapse duration generate several times the reactivation of a single batch send to everyone, and multi-touch escalation sequences meaningfully outperform one-off sends.

The Five Stages, And Why The Order Matters
01
Data foundation
The member list structured so a system can read it. Every member needs a state, a source, an engagement history, and fields the flows can trigger on. The least visible work in the engagement and the one that makes all of it possible.
02
Segmentation architecture
Your eight-level model and three-state engagement model translated into rules a system can execute. What moves someone from engaged to declining. What triggers a re-engagement attempt and what triggers removal.
03
Journey design
What each type of member experiences over time. The pre, during, and post rhythm for a cohort. The follow-up for an event registrant. The re-permission path for someone who's gone quiet. A strategy deliverable, not a design one.
04
Creative and content
The templates, the copy, the campaign platform, the look and feel. The part that's visible, and the part most people picture when they read "assets." It sits fourth in the order for a reason.
05
Build, test, and deploy
Configuring journeys, connecting data, testing every branch, warming the sending domain, monitoring deliverability, and reading what comes back. Where a lifecycle program becomes a live system rather than a document.

Most of what FSG has asked for lives in stages one, two, three, and five. Only stage four is what a traditional creative scope covers. That's the honest shape of this engagement, and it's why the two structures differ as much as they do.

One Risk, Before You Spend Anything
You could run a perfect campaign and lose the network for six months.

You have roughly 2,300 members who haven't been meaningfully contacted in years. The instinct is to reach all of them at once with something beautiful. We'd advise against it.

Email providers judge a sender by how recipients respond. Sending a large volume to addresses that have gone cold produces low engagement, elevated bounces, and spam complaints, and inbox providers read that as a signal to filter the sender. Industry guidance is consistent: a lapsed segment with very low open history should be warmed through a smaller re-permission sequence before any full campaign runs, and bounce rates above roughly two percent put a sender at risk. Around a quarter of email addresses go inactive or invalid in a given year, which on a list untouched since 2021 or earlier compounds considerably.

The practical consequence is that FSG could execute a technically excellent campaign and damage its own ability to reach the network for months afterward. This is avoidable, and avoiding it is a sequencing decision made before the first send, not a fix applied later.

What this is realistically going to produce, so the goal is set honestly rather than optimistically.

Published reactivation benchmarks for lapsed audiences generally land in the mid single digits to low double digits for a well-run multi-touch sequence, with stronger programs reaching higher. Against a dormant population of roughly 2,300, a strong campaign plausibly re-engages somewhere in the low hundreds of members.

That may sound modest. It isn't. Those are people who already know the program, already trust it, and cost a fraction of what a new member costs to acquire. Reactivating an existing relationship is consistently cited as five to ten times cheaper than cold acquisition, and the probability of re-engaging someone who has participated before runs substantially higher than converting someone who never has.

It also produces something arguably more valuable than the members themselves: a clean, accurate picture of who's actually there. Which is the thing you need before the platform decision in the fall.

Where The Money Goes — Minimum Viable Versus Elevated

Not every component needs to be built at the same level. Here's what shifts as investment moves inside each range, so you can spend where it matters and hold the line elsewhere.

01
Audience Strategy & Messaging

Minimum viable: the audience defined and characterized across the three engagement states, messaging translated from your April refresh and the June messaging work into campaign voice, and a message architecture the campaign and every template runs against.

Elevated: the full eight-level network model characterized with messaging tailored to each, the October survey instrument designed to place members correctly, and a positioning point of view on how Expanding Equity should sound now that FSG can be named as managing partner.

Where we'd spend: here, first, before anything else. This is the cheapest component and the one that determines whether the others work.

02
The Welcome Campaign

Minimum viable: one campaign platform covering both movements. Refreshed brand-aligned email design, a three-touch reactivation sequence segmented against the three engagement states, the catalog announcement that lets members sign up for the full year at once, and external assets adapted from the internal movement with a simple referral toolkit.

Elevated: multi-channel including the social presence now available under the loosened co-branding, custom imagery and art direction rather than adapted assets, a five to seven touch escalating sequence, tested variants so the October iterations are informed by real September performance, and a landing experience routing external interest into the catalog.

Where we'd spend: here, second, and specifically on the escalating sequence. That's where the published performance difference actually shows up, and it costs less than the imagery does.

03
Communication Assets & Templates

Minimum viable: a newsletter and digital digest template rebuilt shorter and more scannable, cohort in-between templates, and event registration templates, documented so your team can run them without us.

Elevated: a full journey set per offering type covering active cohort members, event registrants, and inactive members, with automated triggers, data hygiene rules, engagement measurement, and Ghost Note writing the first cycles before handover.

Where we'd hold, for now: you said this has more runway. We agree. The minimum version this year, revisited once the platform decision lands and the renewal is clearer.

04
Implementation & Management

This phase doesn't have a minimum and an elevated version in the same way. It's either in scope or it isn't, because a half-built CRM is worse than no CRM.

What moves it inside its range is the state of the data at transfer and how much of the eight-level model you want executed in year one. Against your three-state model it sits at the lower end. Against all eight levels, with the October survey feeding it, it moves up.

Our recommendation: three-state this year. Build the eight-level model once September and October have told you how this network actually behaves, rather than guessing at it in August.

The campaign itself is roughly half of Campaign Design and remains the largest single line in either structure. That's deliberate. It's the piece your network actually experiences, the piece that has to land in September, and the piece the renewal conversation will be judged on.
